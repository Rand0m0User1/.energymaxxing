# Submission summary

**The Length of the Night** is a 3D city you power with solar, wind, batteries, nuclear (fission) and gas. You change the season and the time of day, or move the city to the Moon's equator or pole, and see when the lights go out.

**What it teaches, in five short tours:**
1. **Solar, night and winter.** Solar is the cheapest power to build, but it stops at night. Batteries carry a summer night, not a long winter one. Always-on power is insurance, sized to the length of the night.
2. **Batteries.** Four battery types (LFP, NMC, sodium-ion, lead-acid) and their trade-offs. Four improvements ahead: cheaper to build, longer life, long-duration storage, more diverse supply. Batteries move energy in time; they cannot make it.
3. **Fission and the nth reactor.** The first reactors cost the most. Building the same design again and again can bring the price down. Fission works today, while fusion has not yet put power on a grid.
4. **Cheap power and industry.** An ammonia plant, an aluminium smelter, a data center and a chip fab each respond differently to cheaper power. The industries that win most are the ones where energy is a big cost and the work can follow cheap hours.
5. **The Moon and orbit.** A two-week night at the Moon's equator, a mostly sunlit pole, NASA's work on small fission reactors, and why solar alone is enough in orbit.
